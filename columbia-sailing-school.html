Summary

Is Columbia Sailing School a fit for Public Record? Not today.

Public Record is built for charities with $250,000 to $2 million budgets in youth, arts, education, human services, or jobs that run on city, state, or federal contracts. Columbia is the right size, about $1 million in 2024, but it runs on lesson fees and has no government money on record. No open government notice funds sailing instruction this week.

What the record says for Columbia: the money for sailing in Chicago comes from family foundations and donor funds, and it is going to the clubs and programs around you.

  • Size and staff: 61 employees, one paid Sailing Master, and a volunteer board under a Commodore.
  • Public money: none from the city or federal government. The record shows small gifts from the PayPal Charitable Giving Fund ($5,464 in 2024 and $5,590 in 2023).
  • Sailing money in Illinois: 135 grants totaling $1,489,634 went to other Illinois sailing, rowing, and boating groups in the funders' 2022 to 2024 returns. The Chicago Yacht Club and its foundation, Judd Goldman Adaptive Sailing, and the rowing programs lead.
  • Where to start: a scholarship cohort for Chicago youth is the shape we would build first. Most of the 141 grants are general support (37) or sailing instruction (13). Captain Dave's Foundation and the Wilson family foundation back the Chicago Yacht Club, and Judd Goldman's funders give for adaptive sailing.
